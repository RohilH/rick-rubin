## What it is
A melody-led genre. Ryos: the lead is first in the loudness order, and it gets width, brightness and body; the
track is built outward from a chord progression and a hook, written on a piano over a drone before any sound
design. One progression usually serves the whole track, and one rhythm (the melody's) runs the drop.

## Tempo, key, harmony
- **Tempo.** 128 BPM in both Ryos demos and his breakdown track; 130 for the KSHMR remix; 126 for Moonboy's
  melodic house; 128 for Alex Rome's remakes and student track.
- **Chords.** Triads in key, opened up: root and fifth low, the third an octave higher (all three creators), with
  the top note of each chord chosen so the tops make a line. Ryos's demo: four chords in one shape (root, fifth,
  octave, tenth), one left without its third, the second and fourth arriving an eighth early
  ([card](../techniques/harmony/ryos-pg-four-chords-one-shape-pushed.md)). Moods and voicing moves:
  [ten progressions](../techniques/harmony/alex-rome-ten-progressions-overview.md). For a dark track, drop the
  thirds ([card](../techniques/harmony/ryos-b-dark-chords-drop-the-third-pick-the-top-note.md)).
- **One progression** for break and drop; a vocal song may change chords before the hook, and the hook's chords
  are then the drop's ([card](../techniques/harmony/ryos-b-one-progression-for-break-and-drop.md)). Every part is
  taken from it ([card](../techniques/harmony/moonboy-b-one-progression-feeds-every-part.md)).
- **Melody.** A rhythm that repeats while the pitches change: bars 1 and 3 alike and 2 and 4 alike, or three alike
  and a fourth that breaks ([symmetry](../techniques/melody/ryos-melody-rhythm-symmetry.md),
  [two rhythm cells](../techniques/melody/ryos-pg-two-rhythm-cells-melody.md),
  [climbing sequence](../techniques/melody/ryos-c-one-bar-cell-climbing-sequence.md)). A melody that carries its
  own harmony: [inline melody](../techniques/melody/alex-rome-c-inline-melody-top-line-plus-harmony-line.md). His
  test of a hook: it is still in his head after a break away.

## How the bass relates to the melody
- If the melody is rhythmic but not fast, the bass plays the chord roots in the melody's rhythm, with pitch-bend
  slides into the changes; every bass layer and the dance piano copy that rhythm (Ryos, EP 50).
- If the melody is fast, everything else slows down: fewer chords, one held bass note per bar with a pitch dive at
  the end, no dance piano ([one fast part](../techniques/arrangement/ryos-c-one-fast-part-everything-else-slower.md), EP 51).
- Melodic house (Alex Rome): held roots on a short dark pluck re-struck in eighths, with passing notes where the
  melody rests ([arp bass](../techniques/bass/alex-rome-c-eighth-note-arp-on-a-pluck-bass.md),
  [passing notes](../techniques/bass/alex-rome-c-bass-passing-notes-in-the-melody-gaps.md)).
- More bounce on the same melody: [bouncy drop](../techniques/bass/ryos-b-bouncy-drop-short-notes-octave-jumps-faster-changes.md).

## Arrangement, section by section
Bar counts are from Ryos's playlists (read off the screen) unless marked.
- **Demo shape** (EP 50): bars 1 to 8 chords over the drone; 9 to 16 the melody, a simple bass and a hat loop; 17
  to 24 the build; a 32-bar drop from bar 25 ([bar map](../techniques/arrangement/ryos-pg-break-build-drop-bar-map.md)).
  EP 51: an 8-bar break, an 8-bar build, a 16-bar drop.
- **Full remix shape** (KSHMR remix, 130 BPM): intro 16 bars, break 18, build 8, drop 16, second break 16, hook 8,
  "hands up" 8, build 8, drop. The second half is not a copy of the first
  ([bar map](../techniques/arrangement/ryos-c-remix-bar-map-hook-and-hands-up.md)).
- **Intro**: previews the track's parts filtered, with the full top and bottom held back; the vocal arrives early
  ([card](../techniques/arrangement/ryos-intro-previews-filtered-gets-to-the-point.md)).
- **Break**: opens with the vocal or piano and one or two synths, then grows in stages, ordered by frequency:
  spacious atmospheres, then a thin high arp, then chords where the vocal repeats with harmonies. Something new
  about every eight bars; each break changes at its midpoint
  ([strip the break](../techniques/arrangement/ryos-b-strip-the-break-then-add-in-stages.md),
  [entries by frequency](../techniques/arrangement/ryos-c-break-entries-ordered-by-frequency.md),
  [held sample into an arp](../techniques/sound-design/ryos-c-held-sample-into-a-high-arp.md)).
- **Build** (8 bars; 16 for a vocal remix, vocal over the first 8 and a riser over the last 8): made after the
  drop, mostly from filters. The drop's leads, chords and basses play behind filters, the break's piano and bass
  are filtered away, the snare roll enters late and its pitch rises (which he calls the progressive house sign),
  risers are chosen by role, and the leads stop half a bar early
  ([build by filtering](../techniques/arrangement/ryos-pg-build-by-filtering-everything-away.md),
  [build previews the drop](../techniques/arrangement/ryos-c-build-previews-the-drop-bass-included.md)).
- **Drop**: the break copied in with a kick and sidechain will sound too soft; harden it group by group
  ([card](../techniques/arrangement/ryos-c-harden-break-sounds-for-the-drop.md)). Fills at the end of every fourth
  bar, a bigger one every eight, the last one quoting the melody's rhythm. The second half needs more than the
  first: an offbeat ride or long hat, shakers, a call and response
  ([every eight bars](../techniques/arrangement/ryos-something-significant-every-eight-bars.md)).
- **Hands up**: a big, floaty half-time section late in the track with sounds saved for it; watch for a kick, a
  sub boom and a bass on one beat ([pick two](../techniques/mixing/ryos-b-kick-sub-boom-bass-pick-two.md)).
- **Transitions**: reverses into hits, hard cuts under vocal phrases, risers that fade in. Effects are large for
  mainstage and subdued for melodic progressive house
  ([card](../techniques/arrangement/ryos-transition-details-reverses-cuts-risers.md)).
- **A simpler skeleton** (Alex Rome, melodic house): one progression as a relaxed 16 bars and an energetic 16,
  something entering halfway through each ([card](../techniques/arrangement/alex-rome-b-two-energy-sections-house-song.md));
  a soft statement of the hook before the loud layers
  ([card](../techniques/sound-design/alex-rome-c-gentle-lead-then-high-energy-layers.md)).
- **Not taught:** an outro, and how the second drop differs from the first (EP 50 leaves it for later).

## Mix and master
- **The lead in front.** In the feedback stream it was not in 10 of 12 tracks. Causes he names: no top-end
  harmonics, reverb as an insert, no body near 300 Hz, width with no centre layer. Fixes:
  [brighten with harmonics](../techniques/sound-design/ryos-brighten-a-dull-lead-with-harmonics.md),
  [reverb on a send](../techniques/mixing/ryos-lead-reverb-send-predelay-duck-glue.md),
  [clear 200 to 500 Hz](../techniques/mixing/ryos-clear-200-500hz-pocket-for-the-lead.md).
- **Lead group**: an exciter, a taste of enhancement, a gentle cut of harshness, a band-limited quarter-note
  delay; no compressor on a stack that is already big ([card](../techniques/mixing/ryos-pg-lead-group-chain-without-squashing.md)).
  A soft piano or weak group in a break is the opposite case: compress it hard
  ([card](../techniques/mixing/ryos-slam-melodic-groups-with-compression.md)).
- **Supporting sounds** are thinned to one band; low mids are kept for the vocal and the drop lead
  ([card](../techniques/mixing/ryos-c-low-mids-set-distance-cut-what-is-masked.md)). A hat goes if the leads
  already hiss ([noise budget](../techniques/mixing/ryos-pg-top-end-noise-budget.md)).
- **Kick and bass**: the sub's duck drawn from this kick's shape
  ([card](../techniques/mixing/ryos-c-sub-duck-curve-traced-from-the-kick.md)); a shaper last on each sustained
  part with its depth automated so breaks do not pump ([card](../techniques/mixing/ryos-pg-pump-depth-by-section.md));
  a buried kick may want a bell near 3 kHz. The bass lands on beats 1 and 3 with the kick when it copies the
  melody, so the duck must cover those hits (the card's inference).
- **Width**: every wide atmosphere and pad narrowed until its correlation stays at or above zero, lows narrower
  than highs ([card](../techniques/mixing/ryos-c-keep-wide-layers-in-phase.md)).
- **Clap**: a wide one with its click shaved so only the kick hits, plus one with a tail
  ([card](../techniques/drums/ryos-c-shave-the-clap-transient-under-the-kick.md)).
- **Master**: a clipper from the first bar with the kick at 0 dB
  ([card](../techniques/mastering/ryos-produce-into-a-clipper-kick-at-zero.md)); his own chain (imager, God
  Particle, later EQ and an inflator) is named, not taught. No loudness figure is given.
  **(inference)** Use the PH references on the shelf for loudness, crest and tonal balance.

## Checking a progressive house track by measurement
- Lead stem above bass and chord stems at 200 to 500 Hz; lead level between phrases not falling to silence.
- Bass and lead onsets coincide when the bass copies the melody; one part clearly busiest when the melody is fast.
- Build: low band falling and brightness rising bar by bar, lowest low band in the last bar, a clear step at the drop.
- Break: loudness and high band stepping up at its midpoint; energy above 8 kHz present; correlation never negative.
- Drop: more near-identical blocks than the references means an 8-bar loop was copied; fix that next.
- Bass group: low band near 0 % side; harmonics from the sub into the mids with no gap.
- Loudness, crest, pump and width inside the range of the PH references. Whether the melody is a hook is Rohil's call.
