# Bass house: what the creators taught

Sources: Moonboy's [bass house tutorial](../sources/moonboy/how-to-bass-house.md) (126 BPM, F sharp minor) and
the second drop of his [melodic house track](../sources/moonboy/how-to-melodic-house.md); Ryos's
[bass house drop](../sources/ryos/bass-house-banger.md) (126 BPM, a drop only) and the "hybrid prog" drop in his
[subscriber fix](../sources/ryos/fixing-subscribers-music.md); Alex Rome's two
[pump-up progressions](../sources/alex-rome/10-chord-patterns-for-10-emotions.md). Lines marked **(inference)**
are the editor's reading. This is knowledge about how the genre is put together, not parts to reuse: every
pattern in the cards is that creator's example.

## What it is
Moonboy: a house beat carrying sounds from other genres. He names festival bass house (dubstep basses), UK
house, ethereal bass house (trance melodies and vocals), speed house and complextro, and mixes them. The emotion
comes from trance in the breakdown; the weight comes from the bass in the drop. Ryos: the melody should be
simple, and the effort goes into sound, production tricks and arrangement; half of his session is ear candy.
What separates it from tech house (Moonboy's sheet): a separate sub, a mid bass that is also the lead, real
melody and pads in the breakdown, and a drop made of two alternating halves.
Sheet: [bass-house-recipe](../techniques/arrangement/moonboy-bass-house-recipe.md).

## Tempo, key, harmony
- **Tempo.** 126 to 128 BPM; both Moonboy and Ryos work at 126. (Moonboy mentions speed house at 150 and a garage
  beat at 120 to 130 in passing.)
- **Harmony.** The breakdown has a real progression; the drop keeps the same notes in heavy sounds (Moonboy), or
  sits on the tonic for half the phrase and moves late ([dark](../techniques/harmony/alex-rome-dark-pump-up-i-vi-iv.md),
  [bright](../techniques/harmony/alex-rome-happy-pump-up-i-iii-vi.md), Alex Rome), or holds one chord
  ([card](../techniques/harmony/alex-rome-tense-one-chord.md)).
- **Sub against lead.** They need not play the same note: the sub a scale third under the bass lead gives a heavy
  drop some harmony ([card](../techniques/harmony/moonboy-sub-a-third-below-the-lead.md)); in the melodic house
  track the sub takes the chords' roots and the lead their top notes.

Detuned saw voices are the base of pad, lead and bass alike (Moonboy): learn that one oscillator setting once.
His rule for a signature sound: find its one characteristic move (for the laser bass, a high-pass that falls at
the start of every note), then pile ordinary effects on it.

## Drums
- Start from the house beat, then five additions for the drop's four-bar phrase: a crash on beat 1, a quiet
  panned ride layer, accent snares on the 16ths around a strong beat, a busier last beat, and a reversed kick
  into the next phrase (Moonboy). Keep the roles, not his positions.
- Ryos gets a simple loop in early, because it tells him how to proceed with the basses, and gives the two halves
  of the drop different drums: a tambourine for the second half, the offbeat ride under the response only, a
  rattle ride layered with the ride.
- In Moonboy's drop the drums are almost empty for the first two bars and run from the B half on (read from his
  lanes; he does not comment). Ryos does the same on purpose as a fake-out.
- A second, heavier kit for a heavier second drop (Moonboy, melodic house).

## Bass
- **Two tracks, two jobs.** The mid bass plays in the mid range and is the lead; the sub plays underneath.
  Ryos's general rule: sub weight near 60 Hz, harmonics above it, and the bass dipping out on the kick
  ([bass in layers](../techniques/bass/ryos-bass-in-layers-sub-harmonics-mid.md)).
- **Low mids.** Bass house can work with almost none, so the sub is felt; Ryos prefers some and adds a meaty
  layer under the call on trial, top cut off and compressed, ready to delete it.
- **The sub's bounce** (Moonboy): held notes, the level ducked hard on every beat, and a pitch-bend spike on
  every offbeat. The triangle wave and tube distortion keep it audible.
- **Two plain layers through one distortion** become one sound: a sine sub and a saw lead on different chord
  notes, grouped, distortion first on the group, delay and reverb last
  ([power-chord bass](../techniques/sound-design/moonboy-b-power-chord-bass-two-layers-one-distortion.md)).
- **From progressive to bass house energy** on the same melody: shorter lead notes, a bass in the lead's rhythm
  with octave jumps, roots changing every bar
  ([bouncy drop](../techniques/bass/ryos-b-bouncy-drop-short-notes-octave-jumps-faster-changes.md)).
- Borrowed in the other direction, a bass house sound can sit over a tech house groove bass
  ([card](../techniques/bass/moonboy-groove-bass-under-a-character-bass.md)).

## The drop, section by section
- **Sounds first** (Ryos): hunt for one "disgusting" lead preset and base the drop on it before writing notes.
- **Two ideas taking turns.** Moonboy: A and B, two bars each (A the laser riff with the sub, B the chops with
  the full drums), from bar 89 of his project: A, B, A, B, then a new block.
  Ryos: a call of a few long notes over two bars, a response of short syncopated hits over the next two, each
  side its own group of layers. ([A and B](../techniques/arrangement/moonboy-a-b-drop-sections.md))
- **Fake-out.** The first two bars hold the lead alone with the sidechain switched off, washed in a rising
  effect; drums and pump arrive on bar three ([card](../techniques/arrangement/ryos-b-fake-out-first-bars-of-the-drop.md)).
- **Ear candy** once the core works: vocal cuts, percussion, stabs, synth cuts, a siren, each tried in a gap of
  the riff, tonal ones following the track's notes ([card](../techniques/arrangement/ryos-b-ear-candy-pass-after-the-core.md)).
- **The second time round changes**: an LFO wobble on the call, a one-shot that starts following the sub,
  different drums ([card](../techniques/arrangement/ryos-b-second-pass-variations-in-a-drop.md)).
- **Hear it in context**: Ryos makes a quick filler build before judging the drop.

## The rest of the track
- **Breakdown** (Moonboy): a plucked trance melody over a pad's long root notes, then the same notes held on a
  rave lead in the build. In his project a melody section sits around bar 65, the build at bar 81 and the drop at
  bar 89 (markers on screen): an eight-bar build.
- **Vocal**: in a space of its own, with a copy smeared into a pad underneath. Wash and stutter tricks:
  [stutter vocal](../techniques/sound-design/moonboy-b-stutter-vocal-with-synced-tremolo.md),
  [flutter plus duck](../techniques/sound-design/moonboy-b-fast-flutter-plus-slow-duck-on-one-knob.md).
- **Not taught:** how the build is made (risers, impacts), the intro and outro, anything after the first drop.
  Ryos's build is a placeholder; Moonboy shows none. **(inference)** The progressive house build cards apply
  (filter the break away, preview the drop), and section lengths should come from the BH references.

## Mix and master
- **Levels** (Moonboy's on-screen guide): kick 0 dB, clap 0 dB, crashes -12 dB, rides -16 dB. Cymbals and hats
  are never loud; the rides are auto-panned and are the only wide drum
  ([card](../techniques/mixing/moonboy-auto-pan-rides-and-drum-level-guide.md)).
- **Sidechain.** Ryos: a volume shaper at a quarter note, fully wet, last on the response group, and on the sub;
  its amount is automated to zero for the fake-out. Moonboy routes synths, basses, chops and effects into one
  "Sidechain" bus while drums and vocals go straight to the master (what is on the bus is not shown).
- **Chops**: all in key, compressed together so they read as one sound, then sidechained (Moonboy).
- **Harsh leads**: cut the sub from the lead, distort, a little OTT, then heavy EQ; Ryos shows a wide dip of
  about 3 dB near 3.85 kHz and a resonance suppressor on the group.
- **Width**: detuned basses measure wide in the low end; high-pass the mid bass near 150 Hz and keep everything
  under that mono (the cards' measurements and inference, not a creator's words).
- **Master**: nothing genre-specific is taught. Ryos's general habit applies: a clipper from the first bar, kick
  at 0 dB ([card](../techniques/mastering/ryos-produce-into-a-clipper-kick-at-zero.md)).

## Checking a bass house track by measurement
- Sub stem: nearly all energy below the bass band, level lowest on the kick's steps, no side energy.
- Mid bass and sub do not overlap below about 120 Hz; nothing wide under 150 Hz.
- A and B (or call and response) within about 1 LU of each other: a change of colour, not of level.
- Fake-out bars: no kick-band energy and no beat-rate dips; both present from bar three.
- Fewer near-identical two-bar blocks and more texture changes per minute than a one-riff drop.
- Breakdown at least 3 LU under the drop; loudness, pump and side energy above 4 kHz inside the BH references.
- Whether the lead is nasty enough is Rohil's to judge on a bounce.
