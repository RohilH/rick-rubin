# Tech house: what the creators taught

Sources: Moonboy's 2025 tutorial and its three chapter videos ([full](../sources/moonboy/how-to-tech-house.md),
[drums](../sources/moonboy/tech-house-drums.md), [basslines](../sources/moonboy/tech-house-basslines.md),
[vocals](../sources/moonboy/tech-house-vocals.md)) and his [2023 video](../sources/moonboy/how-to-tech-house-2023.md);
Alex Rome where he names tech house ([melody styles](../sources/alex-rome/5-melody-styles.md),
[weak bass](../sources/alex-rome/weak-bass-fix.md), [chords](../sources/alex-rome/10-chord-patterns-for-10-emotions.md));
Ryos's [bass house drop](../sources/ryos/bass-house-banger.md), whose cards are tagged for tech house too.
Lines marked **(inference)** are the editor's reading, not something a creator said. This is knowledge about how
the genre is put together. It is not a set of parts: every pattern in the cards is that creator's example.

## What it is
Moonboy: house made "minimal and groovy". Three elements are enough: a simple drum beat, a deep bassline, a
catchy vocal. Because so little is playing, the craft is how much rhythm and flavour a few parts carry, and the
creative effort goes into the bass sound and the vocal processing. He names four styles: European, funky, hard,
and a hip-hop or trap blend that begins as a remix of a rap song (the shape of Rohil's current remix).
Sheet: [tech-house-recipe](../techniques/arrangement/moonboy-tech-house-recipe.md).

## Tempo, key, harmony
- **Tempo.** 123 to 128 BPM, and he used 128 (Moonboy, 2023); house is 120 to 130 and he picks 130 for an upbeat
  track (Moonboy, 2025). Alex Rome's deep house student track is at 126.
- **Root.** Low on purpose: D or D sharp "hits the subs hard" (Moonboy, 2023); D sharp and E in 2025. The octave
  is the one where the bass's strongest peak is 40 to 80 Hz (Alex Rome):
  [bass octave](../techniques/bass/alex-rome-c-bass-octave-by-the-analyzer-40-80hz.md).
- **Harmony.** Often one held minor chord, or none: the bass and the sound do the moving (Alex Rome calls a
  single chord the tech house case): [tense one chord](../techniques/harmony/alex-rome-tense-one-chord.md). When
  chords are wanted over a bass that carries the tune, he uses short stabs, not a held progression.

Which melody role drives: the bassline, with an accent behind it (Alex Rome's

## Drums
- The beat is four lanes of good one-shots; sample quality comes first (Moonboy). The groove comes from a few
  ghost hits on weak 16ths, more of them at the end of the phrase, about 6 dB under the backbeat.
- The first section of a drop is eight bars. Shaker and percussion top loop cover its second half, which lifts
  bars 5 to 8 without touching the kick (Moonboy 2023; that the placement is deliberate is the card's inference).
- The kit is one instrument: grouped, balanced, soft-clipped for level, and filtered as a group for fills
  ([drum group soft clip](../techniques/mixing/moonboy-drum-group-soft-clip.md),
  [band-pass fill](../techniques/arrangement/moonboy-band-pass-the-drum-group-for-fills.md)).
- Choose each drum for the mood: a muffled kick and darker clap for a deeper track, a rimshot in place of a clap
  (Alex Rome). A hidden percussion loop adds depth ([card](../techniques/sound-design/alex-rome-c-hidden-percussion-loop-under-the-music.md)).
- Not taught: swing amounts. A 16th swing setting is on Moonboy's bass clip and never mentioned.

## Bass
- **The sound** is three decisions: wave, filter, what moves the filter
  ([card](../techniques/sound-design/moonboy-wavetable-filter-movement.md)). A saw or square with the sub
  oscillator, a 12 dB low-pass, and a one-shot LFO or envelope that opens the filter at each note; mono, fixed
  start phase, some filter drive. Alex Rome's version of the same sound:
  [filter-envelope pluck](../techniques/sound-design/alex-rome-filter-envelope-pluck.md),
  [filtered-wave bass](../techniques/sound-design/alex-rome-filtered-wave-bass.md).
- **The line**, three ways the creators write it:
  1. Offbeat first: a note between every pair of kicks, then double some, move some by a step, pop one up an
     octave, displace one (Moonboy 2025). It stays out of the kick's way by construction.
  2. Rolling: almost continuous on one root, a note a semitone above for dark colour, single 16ths an octave up,
     one gap in the bar (Moonboy 2023). It lands on the kick, so it needs ducking:
     [rolling root bassline](../techniques/bass/moonboy-b-rolling-root-bassline-with-neighbour-and-octave-pops.md).
  3. As a melody: a root rhythm with rests that loops over two bars, doubled with a new ending so four bars
     resolve (Alex Rome).
- Movement without notes: a pitch-bend ramp per bar
  ([card](../techniques/bass/moonboy-b-pitch-bend-ramps-on-the-bassline.md)).
- If unsure whether the track wants a held or a rhythmic bass, write both on the same roots and test
  ([card](../techniques/bass/alex-rome-c-two-basses-sustained-and-rhythmic-then-choose.md)); a rhythmic line
  needs a plucked sound to be heard ([card](../techniques/bass/alex-rome-c-rhythmic-bass-needs-a-plucked-sound.md)).

## Vocal
- Three sources, each with a cost: a hired vocalist, a remix acapella, samples. A borrowed acapella can be
  scaffolding that is swapped out once the track stands
  ([card](../techniques/workflow/moonboy-remix-vocal-as-placeholder.md)).
- Five treatments: hard tune, pitch forced from a held MIDI note (he holds the bass's note), formant shift,
  vocoder, compression last. One chain can give several characters for different sections
  ([card](../techniques/sound-design/moonboy-b-one-vocal-chain-many-characters.md)).
- Stutter and wash effects suit held notes and ad-libs, not fast rap syllables
  ([stutter](../techniques/sound-design/moonboy-b-stutter-vocal-with-synced-tremolo.md),
  [reverb pad from the vocal](../techniques/sound-design/moonboy-b-vocal-reverb-pad-by-resampling.md)).

## Arrangement
What the creators gave is thin: Moonboy's 2025 series stops at a four-bar loop.
- **Everything in eights** (Moonboy 2023): the riff alone for eight bars, a riser section of eight, then the
  drop, whose first section is eight bars. A catchy vocal runs through the whole track.
- **A change every eight bars**, about two per boundary, of four kinds: add a part, remove one, add expression,
  remove expression (Alex Rome's [two-loop rule](../techniques/arrangement/alex-rome-b-two-loop-rule.md); Ryos's
  [every eight bars](../techniques/arrangement/ryos-something-significant-every-eight-bars.md)). Drums arrive in
  stages ([card](../techniques/drums/alex-rome-b-drums-added-in-stages.md)).
- **The drop is two things taking turns**, not one loop: a long-note call answered by short hits, or two
  alternating two-bar ideas ([call and response](../techniques/arrangement/ryos-b-call-and-response-drop-two-groups.md),
  [A and B](../techniques/arrangement/moonboy-a-b-drop-sections.md)). It can start with a
  [fake-out](../techniques/arrangement/ryos-b-fake-out-first-bars-of-the-drop.md), and its second pass changes
  ([card](../techniques/arrangement/ryos-b-second-pass-variations-in-a-drop.md)). Once the core works, an
  [ear-candy pass](../techniques/arrangement/ryos-b-ear-candy-pass-after-the-core.md) fills the gaps.
- **Transitions**: the band-pass on the drum group for a beat, or for a thin two-bar opening, is the only device
  Moonboy shows. General ones: [transition details](../techniques/arrangement/ryos-transition-details-reverses-cuts-risers.md),
  [opposite automation](../techniques/arrangement/alex-rome-b-opposite-automation-handoff.md).
- **Not taught for this genre:** intro and outro lengths for DJs, how a breakdown and build are made, the second
  half of the track. **(inference)** Take section lengths from the TH references on the shelf, and build the
  sections with the two-loop rule, since the parts are few and the changes must be small.

## Mix and master
- **Levels** (Moonboy): kick and clap at the top, ghost snare and crash about 6 dB under, rides far lower (his
  bass house guide says 16 dB under). Cymbals are never loud.
- **Drums**: soft clip the group; a limiter thickens a thin clap. Loud and clean comes from clipping, not
  compressing (Moonboy).
- **Kick and bass**: the offbeat line needs little ducking; the rolling line needs it. A volume shaper sized to
  the kick's body ([card](../techniques/mixing/alex-rome-c-kickstart-on-the-instrument-group-keyed-by-the-kick.md));
  on a held bass the duck is also its only movement (Alex Rome). The groove bass keeps the sub and a character
  layer stays above about 100 Hz (the card's inference).
- **Room**: low-cut everything but kick and bass; nothing wide under 150 Hz; fix the kick and put one melody in
  front before judging a bass ([card](../techniques/mixing/alex-rome-c-one-melody-in-front-before-adding-bass.md)).
- **Master**: a clipper from the first bar with the kick at 0 dB (Ryos, said for any genre:
  [card](../techniques/mastering/ryos-produce-into-a-clipper-kick-at-zero.md)). No creator gives a loudness
  figure; the released tech house measured in this repo sits near -7.5 LUFS with about 8.5 dB of crest.
- **Not taught for this genre:** reverb and delay sends, a master chain, sidechain settings.

## Checking a tech house track by measurement
- Bass energy per 16th: on the offbeats for an offbeat line; dipping on steps 1, 5, 9, 13 for a rolling line.
- Strongest bass peak between 40 and 80 Hz; side energy under 150 Hz near zero.
- Top-end activity rising from bars 1 to 4 to bars 5 to 8 of a drop; one more texture change per eight bars.
- Drum group: kick the loudest step, ghost hits about 6 dB under the clap.
- Loudness, crest, low-end pump and top-end activity inside the range of the TH references.
- Groove, and whether the vocal is a hook, are Rohil's to judge on a bounce.
