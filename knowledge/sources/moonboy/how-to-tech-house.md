## What it is about
A beginner-level walk through the three parts he says a tech house track is made of: a simple drum beat, a
deep groovy bassline and a catchy vocal. He works in Ableton Live 12 with Serum 2 at 130 BPM, builds a 4-bar
loop, designs a pluck bass from Serum's init patch, writes an offbeat bassline, then shows five ways to
process a vocal and three ways to get one. It stops at the loop: no arrangement, no transitions, no master.
About two of the nineteen minutes sell his "Project X" pack.

## The main ideas
1. **Three elements, nothing else needed**: drum beat, bassline, vocal. Because the genre is so bare, the
   craft is in how much rhythm and flavour you get from few parts ([0:21](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=21),
   [0:42](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=42)).
2. **The bass has two jobs, often two sounds**: a "groove" bass that sets the rhythm of the whole track and
   keeps running, and a character sound (acid, robotic stutter, a borrowed dubstep or bass house sound) laid
   on top of it while the groove carries on ([6:06](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=366) to
   [7:07](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=427)).
3. **Every synth sound is three decisions**: the wavetable (character), the filter (how deep or bright), and
   movement (an LFO or envelope on the filter). Master those and most basses are variations
   ([7:07](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=427) to [8:49](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=529)).
4. **The bassline lives on the offbeat**: start with a note between every kick, then double some notes, move
   some by a step, throw one up an octave ([11:57](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=717)).
5. **Drums are treated as one instrument**: group them, balance them, clip the group for loudness, and put
   performance effects (a band-pass filter switched on for a beat) on the group, not on single hits
   ([3:37](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=217), [5:20](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=320)).
6. **The vocal is the hook, and it is processed like a synth**: hard-tuned, played from a keyboard, formant
   shifted, vocoded, then squashed ([13:17](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=797)).
7. **A remix acapella is scaffolding**: write around someone else's vocal to get a vibe fast, then swap it
   for your own once the track stands ([17:34](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=1054)).
8. Styles he names inside the genre: a European style, a funky one, a hard one, and a hip-hop/trap blend that
   drops into dubstep ([0:42](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=42)).

## Step by step
1. **Tempo.** House is 120 to 130 BPM (said); he picks 130 for an upbeat track (said; 130.00 shown at
   [2:20](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=140)).
2. **Core beat** ([2:05](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=125)). Kick on every beat, clap on beats 2 and 4
   ("Clap (Thick)" shown), hat on every offbeat 8th, a crash on beat 1 (all said, shown at
   [2:40](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=160)). Two bars, duplicated to four.
3. **Groove hits** ([2:48](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=168)). On a "Snare" lane: snares on the last 16th
   of bar 1 and the second 16th of bar 2, a "Clap (Snappy)" on the and of beat 4 in bar 2, the same in bars
   3 to 4 plus one more clap on the last 16th of bar 4 (shown at
   [3:12](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=192) and [3:23](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=203); he only says
   "percussive grooves").
4. **Group and level** ([3:37](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=217)). All drums into a group named Drums.
   Snare down to about −6 dB (said); cymbal −6.0 dB (shown at [4:22](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=262)).
5. **Soft clip the group** ([4:19](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=259)). Glue Compressor on the group with
   Soft Clip on (said); threshold 0 dB, make-up 0 dB, dry/wet 100 %: the defaults (shown at
   [4:35](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=275)).
6. **Fatten a thin clap** ([4:39](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=279)). Limiter on the clap: ceiling −3 dB
   (said), input gain up (11 dB shown), release down (0.02 ms shown at
   [5:18](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=318)).
7. **Band-pass the group for fills** ([5:20](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=320)). Auto Filter, band-pass,
   24 dB, around 1 kHz, resonance 31 to 45 % (shown at [5:35](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=335)); the
   device on/off is automated so it switches on for the last beat before bar 3 (shown at
   [5:52](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=352)).
8. **Four bass personalities** ([6:06](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=366)). #1 "The Groove" (piano roll
   shown at [6:26](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=386)); #2 "Sci-Fi Acid" (Serum 2 preset "BASS - Sci-Fi 1"
   shown at [6:38](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=398): one saw with 6 unison voices, MG Low 12 with a
   resonant bump, LFO 1 in Envelope mode at 2 bars falling, mono and legato); #3 "Robo-Stutter" and #4
   "BASS x House" are heard, not shown.
9. **Bass from init, Serum 2** ([7:07](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=427)). Saw (Default Shapes, position
   1), low-pass filter (MG Low 18 while demonstrating, MG Low 12 in the end), LFO 1 dragged to cutoff. He
   redraws the LFO as a falling curve for a stab, changes its rate (1/4 then 1/8 shown) and switches it to
   Envelope mode so it plays once per note ([9:10](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=550) to
   [9:30](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=570)).
10. **Finishing the bass** ([10:14](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=614)). Release up (520 ms shown), mono on,
    sustain down (−18.9 dB shown), decay tightened, Sub oscillator on, cutoff lower, oscillator random phase
    to 0 and phase moved by ear (248° shown), filter drive up (32 % shown at
    [11:44](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=704)).
11. **Bassline** ([11:57](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=717)). Starts on D#0 with a note on every offbeat
    (said, shown at [12:15](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=735)); then doubles notes into two 16ths, moves
    notes up a semitone to E0, throws one up an octave to E1 and one to G#0 (shown at
    [12:48](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=768)). The clip has a "Swing 16ths" groove assigned (shown, not
    mentioned). Three more patterns flash by from his packs
    ([12:57](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=777)): a one-note straight-8th pulse with 16th runs; a
    two-16ths-then-held-note figure with an upward jump; a pulse every three 16ths walking down A, F, D.
12. **Vocal processing** ([13:17](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=797)). (1) Hard tune: Auto Shift, strength
    100 %, smooth 0 ms (shown at [13:48](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=828)). (2) MIDI tune: Auto Shift's
    MIDI input, he holds the bassline's note (shown at [14:18](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=858)).
    (3) Formant shift, −6 % to +24 % shown. (4) Vocoder, pitch-tracking carrier, 20 bands
    (shown at [15:12](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=912)). (5) Compression: an OTT-style multiband
    compressor (shown at [16:08](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=968)).
13. **Where vocals come from** ([16:53](https://youtu.be/JqKaSvHImB0?t=1013)). Hire a vocalist, remix a song,
    or use samples; pros and cons of each.

## Tools the creator uses
- **Ableton Live 12**: Glue Compressor (soft clip on the drum group), Limiter (clap), Auto Filter (band-pass
  fills), Auto Shift (vocal tuning, MIDI tuning, formant), Vocoder, an "OTT" multiband compressor.
- **Serum 2**: the bass, from the init patch. He also mentions Vital presets in his pack.
- **His packs**: "FREE House Drum Kit (Project X Beta)" (every drum hit used), Project X (presets, vocals,
  MIDI; limited to 400 buyers), OMNIA (a bass groove MIDI clip), KULTURE (the acapella "Make That Paper
  Multiply", 130 BPM, A minor).
- **Named for formant shifting**: Ableton Shifter, Soundtoys Little AlterBoy, FL Studio's Edison.
