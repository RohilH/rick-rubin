## What it is about
Ten habits he credits for getting his mixes to a professional level, shown in Logic Pro. Four are technical (low
cuts, a level-matched reference, EQ on reverb, balancing in mono), two are session hygiene (mix on another day,
consolidate layers first), one is monitoring, and three are about getting practice and feedback from outside your
own tracks. The technical ones come with settings on screen; the others are habits. He works in the same DAW and
owns several of the same plug-ins as Rohil, so most of it transfers one to one.

## The main ideas
1. **Headroom is lost in places you do not look.** Low-frequency junk under hats and claps, and full-range reverb
   tails, are what stop a mix getting loud and make the sub muddy ([0:20](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=20),
   [6:13](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=373)).
2. **Compare at equal loudness or the comparison lies.** A reference plug-in that level-matches lets him hear real
   differences in balance (his bass was too loud against the lead, his hats too quiet)
   ([3:07](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=187)).
3. **Width reads as loudness.** Wide sounds seem louder than they are, so he sets the balance with the master summed
   to mono, drums especially ([8:14](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=494)).
4. **Mixing is a separate job from writing.** Different day, tidy project, fewer tracks, less emotion
   ([9:38](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=578), [10:19](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=619),
   [14:12](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=852)).
5. **Get an outside yardstick.** Pay a professional to mix a song you also mixed, and mix other people's songs: both
   show where your own judgement is off ([12:03](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=723),
   [14:12](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=852)).
6. **Hear the truth.** Flat headphones or monitors, not consumer ones ([13:06](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=786)).

## Step by step
1. [0:20](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=20) Low-cut every track except kick and bass. Said: clear what sits in the
   sub region, 40 to 80 Hz. Shown at [1:20](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=80) on a closed hat in Logic's Channel
   EQ: analyzer on, low cut at 176 Hz, 12 dB/oct, Q 0.71; the hat's own energy starts near 200 Hz and peaks at 6 to
   10 kHz.
2. [1:23](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=83) There is paid demand for mixing: he says freelance mixing paid his way
   out of a day job from 2016, without famous credits (SoundBetter, AirGigs, Fiverr).
3. [3:07](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=187) REFERENCE 2 on the Stereo Out, a released song of similar dynamics
   dragged in, Level Match on (shown [4:00](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=240): his mix −17.8 LUFS integrated, the
   reference −14.8, matched). He flips Original/Reference, fixes the bass-to-lead balance, then solos the high band
   of both to compare hats and raises and brightens his hat. The Trinity Display at
   [4:00](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=240) shows the reference brighter above about 2 kHz.
4. [6:13](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=373) EQ the reverb. He solos a snare's reverb and shows it filling Ozone
   Imager 2 and the whole spectrum. In ValhallaVintageVerb (Room mode, 1970s colour, decay 0.62 s, predelay 20 ms;
   shown [7:00](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=420)) he raises EQ LowCut from 10 Hz to 500 Hz (said and shown
   [7:56](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=476)) and lowers HighCut from 20 kHz to about 9 kHz (shown
   [8:06](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=486); "to taste" said). Then sets the mix.
5. [8:14](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=494) Balance in mono: Logic's Direction Mixer (Imaging) on the master,
   Spread down to mono, then go through the faders (said; the plug-in menu is shown
   [9:12](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=552)).
6. [9:38](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=578) Do not mix on the day you wrote it. Sleep, then a dedicated session.
7. [10:19](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=619) Consolidate layers first: three hat tracks doing the same job become
   one, a layered clap is bounced in place (Bounce and Join > Bounce in Place, Ctrl-B; shown
   [11:35](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=695)). Five tracks became two.
8. [12:03](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=723) Mix a song, then hire a professional to mix the same song and
   compare.
9. [13:06](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=786) Studio headphones or monitors. His: Audio-Technica ATH-M50x and
   Yamaha HS8, switching between them.
10. [14:12](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=852) Mix for other people: easier to be logical about a song you are not
    attached to, and you stop chasing details no listener hears.

## Tools the creator uses
- Logic Pro: Channel EQ (low cuts, analyzer), Direction Mixer (mono check), Bounce in Place (consolidating).
- Mastering The Mix REFERENCE 2 on the master (level-matched A/B, band solo, Trinity Display). Rohil owns it.
- iZotope Ozone Imager 2 as a stereo meter on a soloed reverb. Rohil owns it.
- ValhallaVintageVerb, his favourite reverb, with its own EQ section. Rohil has Valhalla Supermassive and Logic's
  ChromaVerb instead.
- Seen on channel strips, not discussed: Serum, ShaperBox, FabFilter Pro-C 2 and Saturn, Battery 4, Quick Sampler.
- Audio-Technica ATH-M50x headphones, Yamaha HS8 monitors.
- SoundBetter, AirGigs, Fiverr for hiring or selling mixing.

## Not covered or unclear
- Tip 1: he says 40 to 80 Hz but the cut shown on the hat is at 176 Hz with a gentle slope. No rule is given for how
  high to cut each kind of track.
- Tip 3: how much he moved the lead and hat faders is not readable; the high-band solo control itself is not in a
  captured frame.
- Tip 5: the Direction Mixer window is never shown, only the menu path; no word on when to go back to stereo.
- The income figures and the claims about what clients want are his own account, not checked.
- Compression, sidechain, saturation and mastering are not covered; he keeps those for his paid course.
