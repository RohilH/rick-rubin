## What it is about
Five kinds of melody sorted by the job they do in a track, each built quickly in Ableton Live with Serum and tied
to genres and released examples: a melody with its harmony built in, a one-note pulse, a background accent, a
melody played by the bass, and the lead of a drop. The names are his own. It is a vocabulary for deciding what a
section's melodic content should be, and for noticing which role a thin track is missing. Everything here is method
and reasoning; the notes he plays are examples, not parts to reuse.

## The main ideas
1. **A melody is defined by its role, not its notes.** Foreground driver (lead, inline, bass, pulse) or background
   dressing (accent). A track can use several at once ([0:00](https://youtu.be/cjyEdyEWXbQ?t=0)).
2. **A top line alone sounds unfinished; a second line under it makes it self-sufficient.** That is why piano-house
   melodies sound big before any chords are added ([1:03](https://youtu.be/cjyEdyEWXbQ?t=63),
   [6:10](https://youtu.be/cjyEdyEWXbQ?t=370)).
3. **The less a melody moves, the more the harmony can.** A one-note pulse accepts almost any chord progression
   under it ([7:35](https://youtu.be/cjyEdyEWXbQ?t=455)).
4. **Background melodies are free.** Because nobody is following them, an accent can wander, be random, and be
   drowned in reverb ([12:23](https://youtu.be/cjyEdyEWXbQ?t=743)).
5. **A bass can be the melody**, and then the chords shrink to stabs ([13:25](https://youtu.be/cjyEdyEWXbQ?t=805),
   [15:38](https://youtu.be/cjyEdyEWXbQ?t=938)).
6. **A lead is written last and many times**, over a drop that already exists
   ([18:16](https://youtu.be/cjyEdyEWXbQ?t=1096)).

## Step by step
1. [0:18](https://youtu.be/cjyEdyEWXbQ?t=18) **Inline melody** (also "embedded" or "chordal"; trance, progressive
   house, much tech house). A top line of repeated notes (shown [0:58](https://youtu.be/cjyEdyEWXbQ?t=58): grouped
   3+3+2 sixteenths, long stretches on one pitch). He copies its rhythm an octave or more lower and picks one
   harmony note per bar by trial (shown [2:15](https://youtu.be/cjyEdyEWXbQ?t=135),
   [3:38](https://youtu.be/cjyEdyEWXbQ?t=218)). Those lower notes then become chord roots: F, G, A minor, C (said,
   [4:28](https://youtu.be/cjyEdyEWXbQ?t=268)). Examples named: tracks called "Sleepless" and "Still Sleepless".
2. [6:20](https://youtu.be/cjyEdyEWXbQ?t=380) **Pulsing drone melody** (Afro house, melodic house). One note in a
   rhythm; he says a dotted-eighth rhythm that resets every two bars; the piano roll at
   [6:58](https://youtu.be/cjyEdyEWXbQ?t=418) shows onsets on sixteenths 0, 3, 6, 8, 11, 14 of each bar. Sound: a
   saw pluck, Serum 2's factory "PL - Alive". He then tries chord sets under it; the caption at
   [8:25](https://youtu.be/cjyEdyEWXbQ?t=505) reads B minor, F# minor, E major, D major. Example: "The Rapture
   Pt.III" (&ME, Black Coffee, Keinemusik).
3. [9:04](https://youtu.be/cjyEdyEWXbQ?t=544) **Accent melody** (any genre). Behind a vocal and chords. A very
   short sound (shown [10:18](https://youtu.be/cjyEdyEWXbQ?t=618): Serum, a saw at unison 2 plus a square an octave
   up, MG Low 12, amp decay 145 ms, sustain −46.8 dB). Live's Arpeggiator in Chord Trigger style, rate 1/16, Hold
   on (shown [10:42](https://youtu.be/cjyEdyEWXbQ?t=642)); he holds scattered notes for uneven lengths. Then
   ValhallaShimmer (shown [11:42](https://youtu.be/cjyEdyEWXbQ?t=702): mix 0.33, shift 12, feedback 0.211,
   diffusion 0.910, size 0.50, high cut 8000, bright), more reverbs stacked, even fully wet. Examples: John
   Summit "Where You Are", Chris Lake "Ease My Mind" (a guitar that enters halfway through the verse).
4. [13:25](https://youtu.be/cjyEdyEWXbQ?t=805) **Bass melody** (tech house; his favourite example is "Faded"). Start
   on a root note, break up the rhythm, leave rests, get a two-bar loop, then double it and change the ending so it
   resolves (shown [15:28](https://youtu.be/cjyEdyEWXbQ?t=928)). Over it: short piano stabs instead of a held
   progression (shown [16:20](https://youtu.be/cjyEdyEWXbQ?t=980)), or chords over a held bottom note.
5. [17:00](https://youtu.be/cjyEdyEWXbQ?t=1020) **Lead melody** (remakes of Avicii's "Levels" and others). Start
   from chords (F, G, A minor, C, voiced as bare intervals), build the drop, loop it, and write many candidate
   melodies. Options he tries: a synth lead, then a vocal stutter: a sustained vocal with reverb, chopped by a
   trance gate (shown [20:12](https://youtu.be/cjyEdyEWXbQ?t=1212): Kilohearts Trance Gate, 16 steps, resolution
   1/32, mix 78 %).

## Tools the creator uses
- Ableton Live 12: piano roll with scale highlighting, Arpeggiator (Chord Trigger).
- Serum 2 (init patch for the inline example; factory pluck "PL - Alive" for the pulse) and Serum 1 (accent sound).
- ValhallaShimmer for the accent melody's wash; ValhallaVintageVerb in the same chain list.
- Kilohearts Trance Gate (free) for the vocal stutter lead.
- Splice-style vocal samples as stand-in vocals.
- A free preset pack of the video's sounds for Serum and for Vital (link in the description; not downloaded).

## Not covered or unclear
- Exact pitches are not legible in the Live piano roll at 720p (no note names on the keys). The pulse note looks
  like F#2 by position, which would fit the four captioned chords; treat that as a reading, not a fact.
- "Resets every two bars" is said; the frame shows the 3+3+2 grouping restarting every half bar.
- Captions garble the names: the inline example's artist, "Faded" credited to Zedd (the well-known "Faded" is
  ZHU's), and the pulse example's title. The played excerpts cannot be heard from captions.
- How he chooses between lead candidates is not explained beyond "does it come to life".
- No settings for the reverbs stacked after Shimmer; the trance gate's ADSR knobs are not readable.
