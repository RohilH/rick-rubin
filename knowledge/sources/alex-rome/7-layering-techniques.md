## What it is about
Seven ways to add layers, each shown on a short example in Logic Pro: splitting a melody by line, building the bass
from the chord sound, a wide bass layer, swapping a gentle lead for loud layers, a narrow pad joined by a wide one,
a hidden drone, and hidden percussion loops. Two examples are remakes of big progressive house tracks (deadmau5,
Avicii with Nicky Romero), so the layering is the kind Rohil is aiming at. The last two techniques are his answer
to "the track is done but sounds thin", which is the problem Rohil describes.

## The main ideas
1. **A layer needs a job.** Each technique gives the new layer something the existing sound lacks: a register, a
   rhythm, width, energy, or atmosphere ([0:00](https://youtu.be/u8ozR9cnCTU?t=0)).
2. **Layers that share an exact rhythm sound like one big instrument.** His bass copies the chords' rhythm note for
   note ([4:18](https://youtu.be/u8ozR9cnCTU?t=258)).
3. **Width is a contrast, not a setting.** A wide layer only feels wide after a narrow one, so he stages them eight
   bars apart ([8:59](https://youtu.be/u8ozR9cnCTU?t=539)).
4. **Only one layer owns the sub.** Every added bass layer is cut out of the 40 to 80 Hz zone
   ([5:20](https://youtu.be/u8ozR9cnCTU?t=320)).
5. **Depth comes from parts nobody listens to.** A drone and a very quiet percussion loop make a "finished" sparse
   section feel full without adding anything a listener would name ([12:14](https://youtu.be/u8ozR9cnCTU?t=734),
   [14:42](https://youtu.be/u8ozR9cnCTU?t=882)).

## Step by step
1. [0:13](https://youtu.be/u8ozR9cnCTU?t=13) **Line layering.** A remake of deadmau5's "The Veldt" at 128 BPM (shown
   [1:12](https://youtu.be/u8ozR9cnCTU?t=72)). The part has three lines (shown
   [1:48](https://youtu.be/u8ozR9cnCTU?t=108): a held top line, a moving middle line, a bottom line an octave and
   more below). Top and bottom go to a second synth that arpeggiates in eighths; the middle line gets its own
   sound; the whole part is doubled on a piano; the bass plays eighths.
2. [2:20](https://youtu.be/u8ozR9cnCTU?t=140) **Bass matching.** Duplicate the chord track, keep only the bottom
   notes so the rhythm is identical (shown [3:25](https://youtu.be/u8ozR9cnCTU?t=205)), remove the EQ and any
   reverb so the bass is dry, and check on Channel EQ's analyzer that it sits at 40 to 80 Hz.
3. [4:30](https://youtu.be/u8ozR9cnCTU?t=270) **Wide bass layer.** Duplicate the bass, add a chorus (Modulation
   menu) with mix at 100 %, adjust by ear, low-cut the sub out of the copy, and check for phasing on headphones.
4. [6:10](https://youtu.be/u8ozR9cnCTU?t=370) **Dynamic lead layering.** A remake of "I Could Be the One". A soft
   sound plays the melody and chords for a few loops; then the melody alone goes to a high-energy lead, the chords
   to an expressive sound with a rhythm, plus a piano, another synth and a second lead layer.
5. [8:20](https://youtu.be/u8ozR9cnCTU?t=500) **Skinny wide.** Serum init saw, filter on, MG Low 24, cutoff low
   (shown as 220 Hz in the automation at [10:30](https://youtu.be/u8ozR9cnCTU?t=630)); one voice, so it is a thin
   line on Ozone Imager. A duplicate with unison 8 and default detune (shown
   [9:42](https://youtu.be/u8ozR9cnCTU?t=582)) plays the second eight bars. Its region starts early and its cutoff
   ramps from 8 Hz up to 220 Hz, arriving on the boundary (shown [10:30](https://youtu.be/u8ozR9cnCTU?t=630)).
6. [11:35](https://youtu.be/u8ozR9cnCTU?t=695) **"The Armin"** (from Armin van Buuren's studio video): a hidden
   drone. Any synth sound (his is from Sylenth1), one to four high notes, an echo, then a reverb with long decay at
   100 % wet; bounce in place; keep whichever slice of the audio sounds best and loop it under the section.
7. [14:42](https://youtu.be/u8ozR9cnCTU?t=882) **Hidden rhythms.** Audition small percussion loops one at a time,
   very quiet, against the music. He rejects one because its low end hit the chords and keeps one whose rhythm
   follows the main sound. Shown at [15:55](https://youtu.be/u8ozR9cnCTU?t=955): a Splice percussion loop and a
   fader at −6.2 dB on the lane below it.

## Tools the creator uses
- Logic Pro: Channel EQ (analyzer, low cuts), a stock chorus, an echo and a reverb, Bounce in Place, automation.
- Serum (1): the narrow and wide saw pads.
- Sylenth1: source sound for the drone.
- iZotope Ozone Imager 2: to see narrow against wide. Rohil owns it.
- Splice percussion loops (file names on screen).

## Not covered or unclear
- The chorus plug-in's settings were not in a captured frame; he only says mix at 100 % and "mess with the dials".
- The cutoff of the narrow pad is said as a number the captions cut short; 220 Hz is read from the automation lane.
- The drone's echo and reverb settings are not shown, nor how loud the drone or the hidden loop sit. The −6.2 dB
  fader at 15:55 may belong to a different lane.
- Which sounds he chose for the Veldt lines and the Avicii layers is not named (presets are not visible).
- Ryos says the opposite about drones: one that cannot be heard is wasted
  (`techniques/arrangement/ryos-fill-the-mid-range-and-atmosphere-bed.md`). Level is a taste call for Rohil.
