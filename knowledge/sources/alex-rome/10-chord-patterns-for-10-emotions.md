## What it is about
Ten short chord loops, each aimed at one mood, built on screen from plain triads and then revoiced. Nine are in
natural minor, one in major. The progressions themselves are ordinary; the lesson is in what he does to them
afterwards: the same three moves (third up an octave, a pedal tone held over every chord, a second sound doubling
one chord tone) and the choice of sound, rhythm and melody decide the feeling more than the Roman numerals do.
Useful to Rohil as a vocabulary for choosing a harmonic mood for a breakdown, not as parts to copy.

## The main ideas
1. **Voicing carries the emotion, not the progression.** Every example starts as close-position triads and only
   "works" after the third is lifted an octave (root, fifth, tenth) and a pedal is added
   ([0:42](https://youtu.be/_yw85rAb6cw?t=42), [4:13](https://youtu.be/_yw85rAb6cw?t=253)).
2. **Pedal tones re-colour every chord at once.** A held scale degree 3, 7 or 1 above the chords turns triads into
   add9, sus4 and maj7 colours without changing the bass ([0:42](https://youtu.be/_yw85rAb6cw?t=42),
   [5:22](https://youtu.be/_yw85rAb6cw?t=322), [11:53](https://youtu.be/_yw85rAb6cw?t=713)).
3. **The melody decides major or minor.** VI–VII–i–VII sounds serious under a melody on the minor tonic and fifth,
   and hopeful under one on the relative major's tonic and fifth, with the chords untouched
   ([2:06](https://youtu.be/_yw85rAb6cw?t=126)).
4. **One chord is a progression.** A single held minor chord is tense because nothing resolves; the bass and the
   sound do the moving ([2:49](https://youtu.be/_yw85rAb6cw?t=169)). This is the tech house case.
5. **Harmonic rhythm is a lever.** The tonic held for half the loop gives drive (7, 8); uneven chord lengths (3+5
   beats, 5+2+7+2 beats, 2.5+1.5+4 beats) give lift and push (5, 6, 10) (shown, see the cards).
6. **Doubling one chord tone on a second sound picks the character.** Double the third for a darker, more emotional
   minor ([8:02](https://youtu.be/_yw85rAb6cw?t=482)); take the thirds out and double the fifth for a harder, more
   open sound ([9:06](https://youtu.be/_yw85rAb6cw?t=546)).
7. **Sound choice is part of the harmony.** "Chill" is mostly the soft piano and a tonic drone; "tense" is the dark
   patch ([9:47](https://youtu.be/_yw85rAb6cw?t=587), [3:10](https://youtu.be/_yw85rAb6cw?t=190)).

## Tools the creator uses
- FL Studio piano roll (progressions 1, 2, 7, 8), Ableton Live (3 to 6), Logic Pro (9, 10) (shown).
- Sylenth1 for the synth chords (shown in FL's title bar at [7:42](https://youtu.be/_yw85rAb6cw?t=462) and Ableton's
  status bar at [3:08](https://youtu.be/_yw85rAb6cw?t=188)).
- Kontakt as the instrument on the Logic strip of progression 10, followed by Ozone Imager, Output Portal, OTT,
  Channel EQ and Decimort 2 (shown [12:32](https://youtu.be/_yw85rAb6cw?t=752)). Portal is the "weird effect" he
  resamples and chops. A neighbouring strip shows OTT, FabFilter Saturn, Channel EQ.
- A vocal loop from a sample pack over progression 1 (shown [1:12](https://youtu.be/_yw85rAb6cw?t=72); the file name
  ends in an F# key tag, the relative major of D# minor).
- He advertises his own theory course; nothing from it is needed here.
