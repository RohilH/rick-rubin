## What it is about
His general song-making system, the "trench method", shown in Ableton Live with Serum 2 on a cheerful
indie-electro vocal from Splice. Three steps: build the trench (the vocal and its core chords), expand it (drums,
melody, bass, each tested against the rest), arrange it (verse, hook, a crossover, a drop). He says it is what he
teaches his least experienced students. The point throughout is order and patience: get the foundation right
and check every addition, and the later steps become nearly mechanical.

## The main ideas
1. **The foundation is the vocal plus its chords.** With no vocal, start from the lead melody. Everything else
   follows from it ([0:20](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=20)).
2. **Harmonise the vocal; do not write a progression.** Use what is already in the vocal: try bass notes under
   it, in its key ([1:24](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=84), [2:06](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=126)).
3. **Spend disproportionate time on the foundation.** Hours of alternatives, all saved, the best kept
   ([2:46](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=166)).
4. **Chords are getting simpler.** Root plus one or two notes with the vocal on top, not the four- and five-note
   stacks of big room or older progressive house ([3:51](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=231)).
5. **Check before you add.** After each new instrument, test it with the others; parts that do not work together
   cannot be rescued by adding more ([7:03](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=423),
   [9:11](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=551)).
6. **Once the trench is done you cannot mess up.** Layering, better sounds and arrangement are then safe
   ([11:48](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=708)).
7. **A song is its transitions.** A loop or a drop is a section, not a song
   ([13:36](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=816)).
8. **A breakdown is a crossover,** and the best build-ups imply the drop
   ([12:54](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=774), [14:37](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=877)).
9. **Presets are fine.** You do not always have to make sounds from scratch
   ([7:45](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=465)).

## Step by step
1. [0:20](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=20) Step 1. A female verse and hook from Splice (file name shown
   [16:00](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=960): a 120 BPM hook in A minor).
2. [1:45](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=105) The chord-writing sound: Serum 2 init, default saw, filter on (shown
   [1:50](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=110): MG Low 24). He calls it the most popular sound in dance music.
3. [2:06](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=126) Bass notes under the vocal in A minor, several attempts on camera;
   thirds on top ([3:28](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=208)); a pedal and the octave raised
   ([4:13](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=253)). Shown [3:55](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=235) and
   [4:40](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=280): four chords over four bars lasting 6, 2, 6 and 2 beats, each a
   bottom note and a note about an octave and a third above, with one held note over all of them.
4. [4:57](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=297) "Sauce": a pitch movement at the start of each chord note, tried
   falling and rising (ENV 2 open in Serum 2 at [5:10](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=310)).
5. [5:58](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=358) Step 2. Drums: a Splice kit in a drum rack, programmed in MIDI
   (shown [6:30](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=390) and [7:05](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=425): 16th hats,
   offbeat open hat, backbeat clap, syncopated kicks from two samples, 16th swing groove). Checked against the
   chords ([7:03](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=423)).
6. [7:45](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=465) Melody: a preset made pluckier; dotted-eighth rhythm; pitches follow
   the chords; second half resolves (shown [9:00](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=540)). Checked again.
7. [9:35](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=575) Bass: the chords' bottom notes; saw an octave down with unison 4,
   filtered to the sub (shown [10:40](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=640), with Kickstart 2 and a reverb on the
   track). One wrong note fixed; chords muted to test bass, drums and melody
   ([11:07](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=667)).
8. [11:48](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=708) Step 3. Verse, hook and drop idea laid out; a crossover inserted:
   high drums only, the lead hinted ([13:16](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=796)); chords removed and bass low in
   the build ([14:37](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=877)); filter automation on every part (shown
   [15:10](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=910)).
9. [15:19](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=919) Playback, then a quieter alternative lead made with students
   ([16:16](https://youtu.be/XlltkalLaU4?t=976)).

## Tools the creator uses
- Ableton Live: Drum Rack, groove pool (a 16th swing), clip automation. Rohil: Logic's Drum Machine Designer and
  region swing.
- Serum 2: chords (init saw, MG Low 24, ENV 2 for the pitch move), bass (saw, octave −1, unison 4), a preset for
  the lead.
- Splice: the vocal, the drum kit and one-shots; a KSHMR cymbal swell for the transition.
- Kickstart 2 on the bass (Rohil owns it); Ableton Reverb. On the chord track, visible but not discussed: Echo,
  EQ Eight, FabFilter Pro-Q 2, ValhallaShimmer, ValhallaVintageVerb.
