## What it is about
A beginner student likes his melody but says the track lacks a professional sound. Alex reworks the same notes in
Logic Pro at 128 BPM, in the order he hears problems: the lead sound, how the chords support the melody, the
bassline, then the drums. Almost nothing is a mixing move. The fixes are sound choice, voicing, envelope shape, note
choice and sample choice, which is his point: the "pro sound" is mostly decided before the mix. The student likes
John Summit, so the target is melodic house.

## The main ideas
1. **The same notes with the right sound are a different track.** He keeps the melody and changes what plays it
   ([0:46](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=46)).
2. **Energy should be continuous.** Gaps between melody notes are filled with delay and reverb, and OTT lifts those
   tails so the level between notes does not collapse ([1:28](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=88),
   [1:53](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=113)).
3. **A common sound becomes yours when layered** with something of a different character
   ([2:34](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=154)).
4. **Chords exist to lift the melody.** Spread them out, add a held note, and give them a decaying shape, because
   "professional" sounds are always moving ([3:41](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=221),
   [4:43](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=283)).
5. **The bass answers the melody.** Bottom notes of the chords, in the octave that peaks at 40 to 80 Hz, with
   passing notes where the melody rests ([6:04](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=364),
   [7:50](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=470)).
6. **Use samples made for your genre until you know why you would not**
   ([8:57](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=537)).

## Step by step
1. [0:46](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=46) Lead. The student's sound is a supersaw, which he calls generic and
   hard to use well. Replacement: Serum init, a saw (shown [1:08](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=68): the "MB Saw"
   table), filter on, MG Low 24, ENV 2 dragged to the cutoff: his "electro pluck".
2. [1:28](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=88) Serum's own delay and reverb (shown
   [1:52](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=112): delay synced, 1/4 on both sides, normal mode, its filter at 849 Hz
   with Q 6.8; reverb on Hall). Then OTT after the synth (shown [2:08](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=128): Depth
   64 %, Time 100 %).
3. [2:34](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=154) Layers: the same notes on a Nexus preset with a guitar-like attack,
   and the student's original supersaw kept as a third layer.
4. [3:41](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=221) Chords. Too low and bunched: he moves the middle note of each triad
   up an octave (shown [4:32](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=272): root, fifth, then the third a tenth above the
   root). He adds one note held through all four chords (shown [4:52](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=292): A#3 in
   Logic's naming, just under the top notes).
5. [4:55](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=295) The chord sound is static, so he lowers sustain and lengthens decay
   (shown [5:18](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=318): his preset "PD Basic Pad", two MB Saw oscillators at unison
   10, MG Low 12, ENV 1 attack 11 ms, decay 6.46 s, sustain at minimum, release 39 ms).
6. [5:54](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=354) Bass notes: the bottom note of each chord for a melodic track, or a
   line centred on the key's tonic otherwise (said). Keep an EQ analyzer open and pick the octave that peaks at 40
   to 80 Hz (shown [6:42](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=402): one peak near 75 Hz).
7. [7:07](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=427) Bass sound: the same pluck, shorter and darker (shown
   [7:22](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=442): init saw, MG Low 24), with Logic's Arpeggiator MIDI effect at 1/8
   (shown [7:44](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=464)), so held roots become eight notes a bar.
8. [7:50](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=470) Passing notes in the bass where the melody has gaps (shown
   [8:28](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=508): long roots, short higher notes late in each chord).
9. [8:45](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=525) Drums. A hip-hop kick swapped for a house kick (shown
   [9:18](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=558): the house sample is about twice as long). The student's hat loop
   stays; he adds the open hat between the kicks (shown [9:42](https://youtu.be/Uazkku5yuM8?t=582)), which he calls
   the second most important drum in house.

## Tools the creator uses
- Logic Pro: piano roll, Channel EQ analyzer, Arpeggiator MIDI effect.
- Serum 1: the pluck lead, the pluck bass, and his own pad preset; Serum's built-in delay and reverb.
- Xfer OTT: after the lead, to lift the delay and reverb tails. Rohil owns it.
- reFX Nexus: one layer of the lead stack (a rompler Rohil does not own; any contrasting pluck does the job).
- One-shot kicks and a 128 BPM beat loop from sample packs (names on screen).

## Not covered or unclear
- Knob values for the delay feedback and mix, the reverb, and the pluck's cutoff and envelope amount are not
  readable; only the delay times and its filter are.
- The Nexus preset is not named. The lead layers' relative levels are not shown.
- The track's key is never said (Logic's display shows its default, C major). Chord names are not given.
- No mixing step is shown: no EQ, sidechain or levels. The before and after differ in sound choice and notes.
- "Shorten it, warm it up" on the bass is done by ear with no figures.
