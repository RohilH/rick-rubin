## What it is about
He starts from nothing but a Splice vocal and four chords, in Ableton Live, and turns the loop into about 32 bars
of arrangement with one rule, the "two-loop rule": repeat a main part along the timeline and change the
arrangement every two repeats, by about two changes. The demo is a deep-house build, each section adding to the
last. He is candid that "what next?" never gets easier for him either; the rule is what he leans on. It covers
building up, not breaking down or ending a track.

## The main ideas
1. **The loop trap is a missing ruler.** Adding instruments to a loop, or pasting the loop end to end, both leave
   you in the loop. A repeated main part gives the timeline a grid to hang changes on
   ([1:48](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=108), [2:09](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=129)).
2. **Change every two loops, about two changes each time.** Too many and too few both fail; two is the starting
   point ([2:50](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=170), [4:54](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=294)).
3. **Only four kinds of change exist:** add an instrument, remove one, add expression to one, remove expression
   ([6:37](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=397)).
4. **The song lives in the transition.** Each join gets a "transition check"; it only has to be a satisfying
   change. Anyone can write a melody and chords; moving from one piece of music to the next is the skill
   ([9:07](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=547)).
5. **Parts stay until they "expire".** Repeating the vocal or leaving the drums alone is fine while they still
   work ([3:30](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=210), [6:58](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=418)).
6. **Build up before you strip back.** Removal becomes a good move only once the track is full
   ([10:10](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=610)).
7. **Hold the big move.** He could open the chords in section 3 and saves it for section 4
   ([8:01](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=481)).

## Step by step
1. [0:20](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=20) A lead vocal from Splice (shown
   [0:30](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=30): tagged 79 BPM, G# minor), sped up, with echo, reverb and a filter.
2. [0:40](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=40) Chords on a deep-house synth: four chords, one bar each, roots E,
   G#, B, F# with one note held on top (he says "F"; the piano roll at
   [1:10](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=70) shows F#). Looped to the vocal's length.
3. [2:09](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=129) The rule: chords as main instrument, six four-bar loops, colour
   coded in pairs (shown [2:40](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=160)).
4. [3:10](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=190) Section 2. Vocal repeated. Change 1: bass from the chords' bottom
   notes on a ready bass patch, notes doubled to follow the chords' rhythm
   ([4:12](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=252)). Change 2: kick and hat, four on the floor
   ([5:14](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=314)). Transition check ([5:35](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=335)).
5. [6:17](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=377) Section 3. Change 1: drum layers, a 16th hat, a rimshot on every
   other kick, a top loop ([7:18](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=438); shown
   [7:50](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=470)). Change 2: counter-melody from two vocal chops, transposed into
   key ([8:21](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=501); shown [9:00](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=540)). Check.
6. [10:10](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=610) Section 4, keep building. Change 1: the chords layered an octave
   up, bass notes removed, on a different sound ([10:30](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=630); shown
   [10:50](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=650)). Change 2: the chords' cutoff opened by automation over about a
   bar into the section ([11:10](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=670); shown
   [11:20](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=680)).
7. [11:30](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=690) Playback of the eight-loop arrangement (shown
   [11:40](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=700): sections of eight bars; one effect sample straddles the join
   into section 3; at section 4 the main vocal stops and a new percussion part starts, neither mentioned).

## Tools the creator uses
- Ableton Live (arrangement view, clip colours, automation). Rohil: Logic; nothing here is Ableton-specific.
- Splice for the vocal, the vocal chops and every drum sound (kick, hat, clap, top loop).
- Serum for the bass and a chord layer (track names "Serum"); Kontakt tracks are present, not discussed.
- On the chord track, visible but not discussed: Thermal, RC-20 Retro Color, Portal, FabFilter Saturn 2. On the
  vocal (shown [0:30](https://youtu.be/ZkRds6iTfhQ?t=30)): Compressor, RC-20 and Echo.
- His own earlier sound-design video is where the bass patch comes from (digested as
  `sources/alex-rome/any-sound-from-scratch.md`).
