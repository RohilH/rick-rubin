## What it is about

A live stream in which Ryos (progressive house and big room producer, works in FL Studio) plays 13 tracks
sent in by viewers and says what he would change. Twelve get criticism; one gets "no notes". The senders run
from two months in to people he coaches, and he says he sizes the feedback to the level: basics for beginners,
"nitpicky" mix detail for advanced ones. The tracks are mostly progressive house and big room, with one bass
house style drop edit and one big room techno track. The value is the pattern: the same five or six faults come
up track after track. Everything below is from the captions (nothing was read off the screen).

## The main ideas

Counts are the number of the 12 criticised tracks in which the theme came up (T1..T13 in order of play).

1. **The lead is not at the front of the mix: 10 of 12.** Dull, thin, washed out or buried under the bass. He
   calls "bass too up front, leads too far back" the trend of the day. Causes he names: no top-end harmonics,
   reverb as an insert, no body around 300 Hz, width without a centre layer.
   [24:32](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=1472), [1:06:50](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=4010),
   [2:38:05](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=9485)
2. **Kick and bass do not carry weight together: 8 of 12.** Wrong kick for the genre (too clubby for progressive
   house, or thin, or poppy), bass ringing at 80 to 120 Hz with no 60 Hz under it, a sub with no harmonics above
   it, bass swallowing the kick, sidechain too shallow. He wants to feel the kick, then the bass, as a push and pull.
   [27:43](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=1663), [51:01](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=3061),
   [3:07:00](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=11220)
3. **Transitions and fills are missing the small details: 6 of 12.** Reverse a hit into a section, cut the synth
   dead under a vocal phrase, add a quiet tonal riser, fade risers in, size the effects to the genre (big for
   mainstage, subdued for progressive house). [23:10](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=1390),
   [37:00](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=2220), [1:24:34](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=5074)
4. **The notes need simplifying or correcting: 6 of 12.** One chord too many, busy stutters, a third sitting on
   top of the bass note, parts out of key with each other, a melody whose rhythm never repeats, parts off the
   grid. [34:40](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=2080), [1:31:45](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=5505),
   [2:25:30](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=8730)
5. **A sound does not fit the genre or the mood the track set up: 6 of 12.** An aggressive clap in a floaty
   break, a warm synth over a nasty bass, a bass house kick in progressive house, a thin dance piano asked to
   carry a solo. [21:30](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=1290), [36:31](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=2191),
   [2:05:45](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=7545)
6. **Bass and chords crowd 200 to 500 Hz, where the lead lives: 5 of 12.** He calls 300 to 500 Hz "prime real
   estate" for any lead and cuts it from the chord and bass groups, often only on the sides.
   [27:02](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=1622), [1:07:45](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=4065),
   [2:16:30](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=8190)
7. **Too few layers: 5 of 12.** A section that sounds big only while the vocal plays needs another chord stack;
   a fat bass plus a pretty lead leaves a hole in the mid range; breaks need an atmosphere bed and a little
   high-end sparkle. [2:01:02](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=7262), [2:19:01](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=8341),
   [2:51:01](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=10261)
8. **Melodic parts are too dynamic to have power: 5 of 12.** Pianos, lead groups, plucks and vocals in breaks get
   "slammed" with compression. His own chains are short: compress, keep the warmth, clear the mud, brighten.
   [19:33](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=1173), [1:20:31](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=4831),
   [3:23:33](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=12213)
9. **The section does not develop: 4 of 12.** His rule is something significant every eight bars; the second half
   of a drop needs more than the first (offbeat ride or long hat, shakers, a call and response); drums should be
   louder and should change. [40:30](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=2430), [2:27:50](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=8870),
   [3:20:40](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=12040)
10. **Loudness is left for later: 3 of 12, plus 2 in passing.** Two files arrive very quiet and one crushed by a
    template limiter. Produce into a clipper or limiter from the first bar, kick at 0 dB, everything mixed around
    it. [10:31](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=631), [1:26:10](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=5170),
    [1:35:36](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=5736)
11. **It takes too long to get to the point: 3 of 12.** Intros where the vocal waits 30 seconds, a build that
    previews the hook late. Picture a DJ mixing it in. [17:30](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=1050),
    [1:58:30](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=7110), [3:01:00](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=10860)

Themes that are not faults but frame the rest:

- **Colour first, tidy second.** The first half of making a track is colouring outside the lines, the second is
  refinement; most senders "have all the ingredients" and are stuck there. Pro level is doing what you already
  know, better. [1:05:50](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=3950), [1:04:20](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=3860)
- **Decide a loudness order** (first, second, third); it tells you what to cut. In progressive house the lead
  is first, and the star gets width, brightness and body. [1:09:45](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=4185),
  [2:24:40](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=8680)
- **Layer by contrast**, never ten of the same saw. [1:19:10](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=4750)
- **Remix songs DJs want to play**; a known song also helps short videos. [48:00](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=2880)
- **Dance music is 4/4.** A remix of an odd-metre song feels shifted unless the percussion anchors it.
  [1:55:30](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=6930)

## Tools the creator uses

- **FL Studio**: his DAW. **FabFilter Pro-Q** (Pro-Q 4 named): analyser, mid/side EQ, "subtle" and "warm" band
  modes for adding harmonics. **FabFilter Pro-L**: the transparent limiter he suggests on the master.
- Master while producing: a stereo imager, then **God Particle**. Final masters add EQ and **Oxford Inflator**;
  **Ozone Maximizer** is kept for the end. For beginners: **StandardCLIP** (or any clipper) and a gain utility.
- Brightening: **Noveltech Character** (he says the "Techno Synth" preset at about half), **Ozone** exciter,
  **Thermal**, **Decapitator**, and a multiband distortion the captions give as "DST".
- Thickening: **CamelCrusher**, **OTT**, **Soundgoodizer**, **Sausage Fattener** as "EDM style" compression;
  **Spectre** for saturating a band; a bass enhancer the captions give as "RBS" (probably Waves RBass) for
  harmonics on a sub.
- Space and width: a **Valhalla** reverb with pre-delay; **Trackspacer** on a reverb return; an imager the
  captions give as "space control"; a correlation and sound-field meter (probably iZotope Insight).
- **Soothe** for harsh dance pianos; a **de-esser** on a brightened vocal; **ShaperBox** for 16th-note volume
  rhythms on pads and vocal chops; **Diva** for a smooth analog pad; **Kontakt** guitar libraries and
  **Guitar Rig**; **Mixed In Key Live** to find a key; his own sample pack (atmospheres, energy drones, a kick
  he calls "basketball kick").

## Not covered or unclear

- Captions only: no frames were read, so no setting is "shown". The EQ moves he draws (a dip near 250 Hz, a kick
  bell at 3 kHz, a tilt shelf) are known only by the frequencies he says; depth and Q are never stated.
- Auto captions garble several names: "DST" (a multiband distortion), "space control" (an imager), "inside"
  (a meter), "RBS"; and artists ("Kashmir" is probably KSHMR; "Velar"; the artist of "Butterflies"). The readings
  above are inferred.
- The submitted tracks were not heard or measured here; the faults are his judgement, not ours.
- For building chords he sends beginners to another of his videos (three melody tricks) instead of teaching it.
