## What it is
Four chords rising by step to the minor tonic, with two held notes above that make the whole loop lean towards the
relative major. His "uplifting, joyful, euphoric" example.

## When to use it
A breakdown or main-room hook that should lift without turning plainly major. It leaves room for a vocal on top.

## How
1. Natural minor; chords on degrees 5, 6, 7, 1, one bar each (degrees said; shown
   [0:38](https://youtu.be/_yw85rAb6cw?t=38) in D# minor as A#m, B, C#, D#m, equal lengths).
2. Open every triad: root and fifth low, third an octave up (said; shown [1:02](https://youtu.be/_yw85rAb6cw?t=62)).
3. Hold degree 7 and degree 3 through all four bars, in the same register as the lifted thirds (said; shown
   [1:02](https://youtu.be/_yw85rAb6cw?t=62): C# and F# just above middle C in D# minor).
4. Put a vocal or lead over it (shown [1:12](https://youtu.be/_yw85rAb6cw?t=72): a vocal loop whose file name is
   tagged F#, the relative major).
5. What the pedals turn the chords into (inferred): v becomes the relative major's maj7 in first inversion (A#m + F#
   = F#maj7/A#), VI becomes add9, VII gains a suspended fourth, i becomes m7.
6. To vary it (ours): bring the pedals in eight bars after the chords; pulse the pedal instead of holding it; leave
   the last bar on VII to hand over to the drop.

## In Rohil's setup
Chords, pedal and vocal on separate tracks. A filtered saw pad or the filter-envelope pluck from the sound-design
cards suits the chords; the pedal can be a quieter, brighter layer. Keep chord roots above C2. Before trusting a
vocal loop's key tag, read it: `rick-rubin notes <dir> samples/raw/<vocal>.wav` (pitches and key fit).

## How to check it
`rick-rubin score <dir> --tracks <chords> <pedal>`: the pedal pitches should appear in every bar and the top voice
of each chord should be its third. Then bounce with and without the pedals and ask Rohil which lifts more.

## Why it works
The bass only ever steps up, and it arrives on the tonic. The two pedals are the root and fifth of the relative
major, so a major colour sits on top of a minor bass line the whole time (inferred).
