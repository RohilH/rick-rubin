## What it is
A minor loop that falls from the tonic, reaches the minor four chord and ends on VI without coming home, with the
tonic note held over the second half. His example for sad songs.

## When to use it
A melancholic breakdown, an intro pad, or under a sad vocal. It suits slow harmonic rhythm.

## How
1. Natural minor; degrees 1, 7, 4, 6, one bar each (said; shown [4:00](https://youtu.be/_yw85rAb6cw?t=240) in G
   minor: Gm, F, Cm, Eb).
2. Third of every chord up an octave (said; shown [4:22](https://youtu.be/_yw85rAb6cw?t=262): G–D–Bb, F–C–A, C–G–Eb,
   Eb–Bb–G).
3. Add a pedal on the tonic above the chords (said; shown [4:36](https://youtu.be/_yw85rAb6cw?t=276): G held over
   the Cm and Eb bars, where it is the fifth of iv and the third of VI).
4. He voices it low, about an octave and a half under middle C (shown; Ableton octaves 1 to 2).
5. To vary it (ours): hold the pedal over all four bars (it becomes the ninth of VII); end on i every second pass
   for release; swap iv for a major IV once to let light in.

## In Rohil's setup
His register is below this project's rule that only kick and bass live under C2, so move the chords up an octave
whenever a bass plays. A soft, dark patch fits: the filtered saw pad card with the cutoff well down.

## How to check it
`rick-rubin score <dir> --tracks <chords>`: the tonic should be the top note of bars 3 and 4, and no bar should end
on the tonic chord. Then a bounce against the plain triads for Rohil.

## Why it works
The minor iv is the darkest diatonic chord in a minor key and the loop never resolves, so it keeps sighing. The held
tonic reminds the ear where home is while the bass stays away from it (inferred).
