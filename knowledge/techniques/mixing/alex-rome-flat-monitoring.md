## What it is
Use playback that does not flatter. Consumer headphones and speakers add their own processing so music sounds good
on them; studio headphones and monitors aim for a flat response, so what is heard is what is in the DAW.

## When to use it
Before putting effort into mix skill at all: decisions made on coloured playback do not survive other systems.

## How
1. Get one pair of studio headphones or studio monitors (said).
2. His own: Audio-Technica ATH-M50x headphones, which he notes cost about what consumer headphones do, and Yamaha
   HS8 monitors (said, [13:40](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=820)).
3. He switches between the two while mixing (said).
4. Keep earbuds and consumer speakers for a final check only (inferred; he says they are fine for listening).

## In Rohil's setup
What Rohil monitors on is not recorded in this project; ask before assuming. Whatever it is, the agent's
measurements are the part of the chain that never changes with the room: `rick-rubin reference compare` for tonal
balance, `rick-rubin width` for stereo, so a mix that measures inside the references but sounds wrong to him points
at the monitoring or the room, and one that sounds right but measures bass-heavy points the same way.

## How to check it
Play two or three released tracks from the reference shelf on the monitoring in question. If they sound balanced
there, the playback is trustworthy enough; if all of them sound bass-heavy or dull, the playback is coloured. Then
check his mix against `reference compare`'s tonal balance lines.

## Why it works
Every mix decision is a correction for what is heard. If the playback adds bass, the mix will lack it everywhere
else (said in substance).
