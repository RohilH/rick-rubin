## What it is
His career tip: far more people write and sing than can mix, so mixing other people's songs is both the fastest
practice and a way to fund time for music. He says freelance mixing, started in 2016 with no famous credits, is what
let him leave a day job.

## When to use it
Once Rohil's own mixes hold up against references. Not before: the practice version of this is the card on mixing
other people's songs, which needs no clients.

## How
1. Accept that clients ask one question, whether the mix sounds good, not who you have worked with (said).
2. Build a small before-and-after portfolio from real songs (inferred; he only says proof of skill is enough).
3. List on a freelance marketplace: he names SoundBetter, AirGigs and Fiverr (said,
   [2:46](https://youtu.be/cU0tHoyJYjo?t=166)).
4. Start before feeling ready; he presents waiting for credits as the mistake (said).
5. His income figures are his own account of his own case (said, unverified); treat them as motivation only.

## In Rohil's setup
Nothing to install. The useful part now is the mindset: every mix decision gets written down with numbers
(`rick-rubin reference compare`, `rick-rubin verdict`), which is exactly the before-and-after record a portfolio or
a client conversation needs later.

## How to check it
Not measurable. The honest gate is the reference comparison: when `rick-rubin reference compare <dir>` shows his own
mixes inside the range of released tracks on loudness, tonal balance and width without special pleading, the skill
is saleable.

## Why it works
Time spent mixing many different songs is concentrated practice on one skill, and payment forces finishing
(inferred).
