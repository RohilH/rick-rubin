## What it is
Ryos's picture of how a track gets made: like a page in a colouring book. For the first half you colour freely,
even outside the lines. The second half is refinement, making it tidy. Most submissions in this stream had "all
the ingredients" and were stuck in the second half.

## When to use it
When deciding what to work on next. If the idea is not there yet, do not mix. If the idea is there, stop adding
ideas and start the list below.

## How
1. First half: get good sounds, a mood and a structure down without judging the mix (said,
   [1:06:02](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=3962)).
2. Second half: refinement. For an advanced producer this is the whole gap to a professional result, and it is
   "doing the things you already know, but better", which means nitpicking small details (said,
   [1:04:20](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=3860), [2:26:40](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=8800)).
3. Decide the hierarchy before touching an EQ: which part is loudest, second, third. That order says what to
   cut and from what (said, [1:09:45](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=4185)).
4. Keep the chains simple. He says he keeps coming back to what works: compress, keep warmth, clean mud,
   brighten; complicated techniques are rarely the answer (said, [1:21:20](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=4880)).
5. Match the feedback to the level. A beginner fixes key, loudness and one or two sounds; mid/side cuts and
   reverb ducking are for when the basics hold (said, [3:01](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=181)).
6. Know the context of what you are making: the same note (bigger effects, smaller effects) is right in one
   genre and wrong in another (said, [55:30](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=3330)).
7. Early on, a stock-sounding library part (a strummed guitar) is fine; later, program your own patterns and
   process it (said, [1:41:40](https://youtu.be/3I7jJyVczPQ?t=6100)). He made out-of-key music for years.

## In Rohil's setup
This maps onto how rick-rubin already works. The colouring half is exploration (`skill/explore.md`,
PREFERENCES section 3): alternatives written and compared. The tidying half is the finished-track checklist
card run on a bounce, one change at a time, each read back (`rick-rubin logic ...`) and each followed by a
before and after for him to hear. Rohil is early, so step 5 applies: key, loudness, the kick and the lead come
before any mid/side work. Write what he decides with `rick-rubin verdict` so the hierarchy for a track is on
record.

## How to check it
Not measurable directly. A proxy: the list of open items from the checklist card gets shorter between bounces,
and `rick-rubin reference compare` lists fewer lines outside the references' range each time.

## Why it works
Judging the mix while the idea is still forming kills ideas, and adding ideas while mixing never ends; splitting
the two lets each be done properly.
